Migration answer

What moves from Como
to OPN?

Most of the commercial and operational ticketing core can move to OPN without reproducing Como's implementation. The work that remains is concrete: the buyer seat map, an Italian fiscal issuer boundary, holder and access evidence, and a handful of operator decisions.

Read this page if you need to set scope, estimate a migration or decide what should not be rebuilt. You can use the full 111-row matrix later to test a specific feature, rather than reading it cover to cover.

01

The answer in one page

There is no need to port every Como route or screen. Treat the migration as a reuse, build or decide exercise.

OPN verdictWhat it means in the audit
HasA genuine functional counterpart exists in OPN.
PartialA mechanism exists, but the scope or semantics differ. The gap has to be named before it is planned.
MissingThe audit found no counterpart in Entrails, Vuescera or Vuejones.
n/aThe Como item is dead code, static content, a form, or PlaNet plumbing with no direct OPN equivalent.
OpenThe answer depends on operator, commercial or provider evidence rather than more source reading.

The labels are there to keep the discussion honest. A PlaNet call, static configuration and an embedded JotForm are not the same product surface. Twenty-three of the inventoried capabilities are JotForms with manual follow-up, so their absence in OPN is a product choice, not automatically a regression.

02

What the comparison means in practice

Each capability should be put into one of three buckets before it becomes a migration task.

OPN already has the stronger commerce and operations core in several places. It has a server-validated resale market, a transactional order and ledger flow, dedicated organiser tooling, a waiting room, scanning and automatic seat allocation. It should be the base, not a blank canvas.

03

What we can decide now

These conclusions are based on the audited source, not on a promise that every Como workflow has a direct OPN screen.

OPN already has a broader platform core

It supports multi-brand tenancy, a separate organiser dashboard, server-validated fan resale, a transactional order and ledger flow, scanning and an automatic seat-allocation capability.

Some Como features are manual today

Twenty-three inventoried capabilities are embedded JotForms, mostly hospitality and experience enquiries. The cutover decision is whether to keep the form and process, or productise the workflow.

Seat experience is a product decision

Como supports a buyer clicking an exact stadium seat. OPN has allocation and organiser seating tools, but not that consumer click-to-pick map.

Resale is not a baseline to copy

OPN has the more complete resale design. In the audited Como version, the buyer path is unreachable and the issuance step is a stub.

04

Questions that set the scope

These are the decisions that source review cannot make on its own.

Who remains the certified issuer?

If PlaNet and Alfi remain in that role, OPN needs a well-defined issuer adapter. If not, the work becomes a recognition and compliance programme, not a normal feature build.

What data must migrate?

Issued fiscal titles, seals, holder and seat records, supporter data, season rights, gift-card balances and loyalty balances have different fidelity and liability questions.

Which manual operations continue?

Hospitality, refund requests, accreditations and seasonal campaigns currently depend on forms and staff follow-up. The target service needs a clear owner for each retained process.

What is required at the stadium?

Physical box-office printing, access integration, reporting and any live operational jobs should be confirmed with the people who run match days.

Useful planning rule

Do not create a migration task for a Como feature until someone can say whether it is a retained commercial requirement, a manual workflow worth productising, or a PlaNet bridge that disappears once the target boundary is in place.
